The Medallion System

Three Tiers, One Seamless Experience

Electric Planet uses three types of medallions — an eye-catching Entry Medallion at every trailhead, affordable Trail Medallions deployed by the dozen along paths, and a premium Collector's Bronze Medallion awarded to partners who scale to 100+ trail installations. Each QR code links directly to rich YouTube video content. No app needed — just scan and watch.

GATEWAY

Entry Medallion

The showpiece. A large, decorative 4–5 inch cast aluminum medallion installed at trailhead kiosks, park entrance signs, nature center lobbies, and campground welcome areas.

  • 4–5" diameter, cast aluminum with detailed embossed artwork
  • River scenes, forest motifs, and mountain landscapes unique to each site
  • QR code linking to the Electric Planet YouTube welcome video for the site
  • Electric Planet logo, site name, and medallion number engraved on rim
  • One per site — the "welcome" piece that introduces the experience
TRAIL MARKER

Trail Medallion

The workhorse. A compact 1½" cast aluminum disc designed for mass deployment along trails, riverbanks, geocaching waypoints, and nature paths.

  • 1½" diameter, lightweight cast aluminum — built for volume
  • Laser-etched QR code linking to a unique YouTube narrative experience
  • Clean design with Electric Planet logo — weather-resistant, UV-stable
  • Mounted on trail posts, railings, boulders, and geocache waypoints
  • Priced near cost (~$5/unit) — the real value is the YouTube content

Narrative Modes

Three Ways to Experience Every Story

Every medallion unlocks three distinct YouTube narrative videos — so every visit feels new.

Field Notes Mode

Straight science, beautifully told. Field Notes mode delivers real ecological data, species information, and environmental science through clear, engaging YouTube videos written for curious minds of all ages.

Example — Medallion #4, Piscataquog River

"The rocks beneath your feet are 400 million years old — older than dinosaurs, older than trees. They formed when this spot was at the bottom of an ancient sea."

Character Quest Mode

Follow a character through the landscape. A fictional guide — an animal, an explorer, or even the river itself — narrates the science through story. Think Magic School Bus meets nature documentary.

Example — Same Medallion

"I'm Granite — yes, the rock. I've been here for 400 million years, and let me tell you, I've seen some things. When I was young, I was mud at the bottom of a warm, shallow sea."

Futures Mode

What could this place look like in 50 years? Futures Mode invites explorers to imagine science-based scenarios — from climate adaptation to ecological restoration — turning real data into speculative storytelling.

Example — Same Medallion

"It's 2076. The river is wider now. Climate scientists predicted this — more intense rain events pushing more water through the channel. But look at the banks: community volunteers planted deep-rooted native willows here in 2030."

Flexible for Every Context

Modes are selected by the visitor via the YouTube playlist menu after scanning. Parks and educators can also set a default mode link for field trips and guided programs.

Our Story

Electric Planet was founded on a simple observation: people are more curious outside. But too often, outdoor education feels like a lecture transplanted to a trail. We wanted something different — experiences where the place itself becomes the teacher, where real science is delivered through story, and where every visit reveals something new.

Our three-tier medallion system makes this possible. Decorative Entry Medallions welcome visitors at trailheads, affordable Trail Medallions are deployed by the dozen along paths, riverbanks, and geocaching waypoints, and the prestigious Collector's Bronze Medallion rewards partners who commit to large-scale installations. Every QR scan links to rich YouTube video content — place-based science storytelling that blends ecology, geology, hydrology, and climate science with narrative craft.

Our business model is simple: medallions are distributed at near-cost because our revenue comes from YouTube — ad revenue, channel memberships, sponsorships, and branded content partnerships. The more medallions in the wild, the more explorers discover our content. Parks get beautiful infrastructure for almost nothing, and we build an audience of millions of curious minds.
